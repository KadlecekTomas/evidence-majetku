# Product and UX principles

1. **Attention over analytics.** The home screen answers “what needs action?”; vanity counts belong in reports.
2. **Scan is a primary action.** On mobile it is always one tap away.
3. **Continuous beats confirmatory scanning.** Validation happens in the background; the scanner does not stop after every successful item.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** Teachers see operational facts and actions. Accounting detail is shown only to roles that need it.
5. **One physical object, one permanent QR.** Mutable information never needs a new sticker.
6. **Rooms are first-class objects.** A room QR gives immediate context and supports inventory/move workflows.
7. **Offline is a normal state, not an error state.** Inventory work must survive weak school Wi-Fi.
8. **GORDIC complexity stays behind the product boundary.** Users see synchronised / needs review, not SOAP/XML internals.
9. **No silent conflicts.** If two systems disagree about a governed field, a human-readable resolution task is created.
10. **Every screen must earn its navigation slot.** Documents, warranties and service details belong to asset context unless evidence shows they need a global workflow.

## Visual direction

- quiet neutral surfaces, high contrast text, one primary blue accent
- colour communicates status, not decoration
- 10–16 px radii; restrained shadows
- dense enough for administration, spacious enough for touch
- mobile workflows are designed independently from desktop tables
- no decorative gradients, gamified school motifs or dashboard-card inflation

## Product metric

For operational actions, optimise the time from **unlocking the phone to recording the real-world action**. Click count is secondary to elapsed time and error rate.
